Plate 07 Beginning East Azerbaijan, Iran 38.70° N, 46.55° E

QaraDag

I was born in Qaradag, a region of Iranian Azerbaijan held on every side by mountains.

  • Look left and there are mountains. Look right, ahead or behind, and there are more, some bigger than the others. They make you wonder whether what is up there is the zirve, the summit.
  • A mountain leaves you two choices: surrender or fight. If you surrender, you know the end is close. So the mountains make two kinds of people: the savaşanlar, the ones who fight, and the ones who give up.
  • That Qaradag energy shows in some of the people of these mountains. Babak held them for twenty years against the invading Abbasid armies; his fortress still stands in Qaradag and is seen as a symbol of freedom.
  • Sattar Khan, the son of a Qaradag family, held Tabriz for Iran's constitution in 1908–09, against the Shah's armies and the Russian power behind them.

Bearings carried forward

Mountains make you either surrender or fight. Qaradag raises savaşanlar.

The Selection Pressure: How Cost Decides Which Value Chains Survive

A value chain is the path a human want travels from unmet to met, and at every link sits a cost. The want is the constant; cost does the selecting; and when one cost falls, the rent relocates to whichever cost has not yet fallen.
