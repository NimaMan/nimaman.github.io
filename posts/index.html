The Selection Pressure: How Cost Decides Which Value Chains Survive

Every value chain is the path a human want travels from unmet to met, and at every link sits a cost. Chains evolve by selection: the cheaper, faster way of serving the same want survives and becomes the next link. The want is the constant; cost is the agent of change; the rent always relocates to whichever cost has not yet fallen — including, at the end, the human ones.

The Same Journey, Everywhere: How Value Chains Evolve

Knowledge went from the library to the search box to conversation. Music went from owning records to streaming, and is one step behind. Run the same test across fourteen industries and the same bend appears in every one — own, then retrieve, then participate. The first two steps are nearly universal. The third is decided by two questions: does using it make it, and do you steer or do you wish?
