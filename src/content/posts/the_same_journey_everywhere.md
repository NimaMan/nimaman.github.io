Knowledge seems to have made a neat journey: from what a person remembered, to books and libraries, to search, to a machine that can answer back. Music can be made to look similar: live performance, records, radio, streaming, generation. Put the two beside each other and it is tempting to name three stages, give each one a colour, and send every other industry through them.

That temptation produces a clean table and a false history.

A map is not a song. A meal cannot be copied like a photograph. A robotaxi changes who drives, not merely how a ride is found. Wikipedia changed how an encyclopedia is made years before answer engines changed how it is presented. In film, the evolution of *watching* and the evolution of *making* have separated into different branches. The more industries we add, the less honest a universal sequence becomes.

What the fourteen industries share is not a sequence of stages but an order of explanation. Human nature changes slowest. It produces wants, and each want meets obstacles. Technology attacks the obstacles, changing what can be done and what it costs; when one falls, the scarce thing moves to the next. The value chain (who does the work, who controls it, who is paid) reorganizes around the want each time. The routes differ. The reasoning that explains them is the same everywhere.

**The want is the constant. The chain is what moves.**

## Human nature changes slowest

Start with the creature using the chains. A person in 1850 wanted to know things, get somewhere, eat well and trust a stranger in trade about as much as anyone does now. The tendencies below have long precedents. They are not universal laws or forecasts, and they vary across people, cultures and occasions.

- **Orientation.** We want to know where we stand: where we are, what is true, whom to believe.
- **Pattern and play.** We respond to rhythm, rules and surprise, and enjoy the familiar changed in an interesting way.
- **Feeling.** We use sounds, stories and images to express what we feel, and to change it.
- **Belonging and identity.** We coordinate and belong, become what we practice, and want to be seen by someone who matters.
- **Attachment.** We attach to particulars, such as a song, a recipe or a photograph, and, as the [IKEA effect](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2011.08.002) suggests, to what we made and finished. A similar substitute may not serve the same want.
- **Continuation.** We learn by taking part, and we build on what others left.
- **Trust.** We exchange with strangers when something lets us trust them.
- **The body.** We must be fed, moved and sheltered, and no amount of information does that for us.

One proposition runs through all of them:

> Humans seek effective influence over their internal state and external world. They prefer paths that reduce unwanted effort, delay, and uncertainty—but they value effort that produces agency, competence, identity, connection, or meaning.

The [effort paradox](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477776/) is precisely that effort is both avoided as a cost and sought as a source of value. A person may want a machine to plan a commute but spend ten years learning the piano; one household wants dinner delivered while the next cooks for the pleasure of it. Nor are these two kinds of people: the same person moves between ease and chosen effort, receiving and acting, welcoming help and wanting the result to be recognizably their own.

Music shows how one activity bundles several tendencies. “Listening to music” contains at least three responses:

- **Moving with it.** Rhythm, bass and syncopation can solicit bodily movement; work on [groove](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21767048/) and [syncopation](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0094446) suggests that the pleasure is partly in the invitation to act.
- **Watching it be made.** The pianist’s hands or a DJ reading a room can compel in something like the way skilled sport does; [action-observation research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15616133/) finds that watching skilled movement engages some of the observer’s own motor system, especially when the skill is familiar.
- **Listening privately.** Music regulates mood and arousal, helps construct identity and creates social connection: the [three broad functions](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00511/full) that survived an empirical reduction of people’s many reasons for listening.

These responses may be related or partly independent. Live performance could serve all three at once; the recording served private listening; generative tools now alter making. None is simply “the next version” of the others. **One activity can serve several wants, and technology often serves them separately.**

From here on, three kinds of statement stay apart. The tendencies are observations, with evidence linked where a claim is specific. The wants are inferences, my reading of what each chain is for. Judgments about where a chain stands and what is now scarce are this essay’s claims, and they can be wrong.

## The wants, and what stands in their way

A value chain is the path a human want travels from unmet to met ([The Law](/posts/the_selection_pressure)). What people value, such as arriving, understanding or being fed, is a value system, and it endures. How work, control and rewards are organized to serve it is a value chain, and it reorganizes. **The value system can endure while value chains reorganize.**

So name each want at the level that does not change. Arriving is the objective; driving is one action that serves it, a steering wheel one interface, a petrol engine one implementation. Each layer changes faster than the one above it. Name the want at the level of the engine and every new engine looks like a revolution; name it at the level of arriving and the history becomes legible.

| Chain | The want, and what stands in its way |
| --- | --- |
| Knowledge | *Understanding, and answers I can trust.* What is known lies elsewhere, and must be found, judged and assembled. |
| Music | *Feeling, moving, expressing and belonging through sound.* It needs players, a place, a shared time and skill. |
| Maps and navigation | *Knowing where I am and how to get there.* The ground is unfamiliar, and conditions change on the way. |
| Software and code | *Exact, repeatable work done as I intend.* Intent must become exact instructions that keep working, and be checked. |
| Gaming | *Play — challenge, mastery and company within rules.* It needs companions and a world worth returning to. |
| Retail | *Getting what I need at a fair price, with confidence.* The thing must be found, compared, trusted and delivered. |
| Video and film | *Being absorbed in a story, and telling my own.* Stories are costly to stage and hard to bring before an audience. |
| Photography | *Keeping a moment, and making the image I imagine.* Capture takes skill, and an image must be believed. |
| Personal transport | *Getting somewhere safely, when I choose.* Distance costs time and work, and the trip must be safe. |
| Encyclopedias | *A trustworthy summary of what is known.* It must be compiled by experts, kept current and trusted to be fair. |
| Food | *Nourishment, pleasure and care, often shared.* Food must be grown, kept and cooked; a body cannot eat information. |
| Money and payments | *Storing value and moving it, with trust.* The claim must be good, and payment must arrive and be final. |
| Travel and lodging | *Going somewhere new, and resting safely there.* The place is unknown, the suppliers many, the hosts strangers. |
| Virtual places | *A place to be and build with others, beyond physical limits.* The place must be built, and must last for everyone in it. |

The right-hand column never names a technology: every want and every obstacle can be stated without one. Technology enters next, as an attack on the obstacles.

The structure of a want limits how far technology can carry its chain. A want answered by information, such as a fact, a song or a route, can ride falling copying costs a long way; a want answered in the body, such as a meal, a ride or a bed, stays bound to matter, work and place. One test keeps these readings honest: a want that cannot explain why an older form survives has been named at the wrong level.

## Technology moves what is scarce

Technology is the principal material driver of every chain in the atlas. It changes what can be done and what it costs, attacking the obstacles, usually one at a time. It does not supply the want or adopt itself: people and institutions decide what is adopted, which practices endure, and who benefits. Ask five questions of any transition:

1. What became possible, or much cheaper?
2. Who adopted it, and under which conditions?
3. Who gained access, and whose work was displaced?
4. What could now be kept, shared or continued?
5. Which constraint remained, and who controlled it?

Across the fourteen chains, four answers recur.

**When one obstacle falls, the scarce thing moves.** Search made finding nearly free and left judging. Streaming made access abundant and moved the scarcity to attention. Cheap capture and generation made images abundant and moved it to belief. Ride-hailing made dispatch cheap and left the driver as the binding cost, the cost robotaxis now attack. Assistance moves the person’s share of the work in the same way: from typing to verifying, from searching to checking, from making to selecting. [The Law](/posts/the_selection_pressure) gives the economic form of the same move: the rent relocates to whichever cost has not yet fallen.

**Old forms survive when they serve another part of the want.** Recording did not end live performance; delivery did not end home cooking; cards did not end cash. Old practices persist for skill, ritual, reliability, ownership or identity, or because people prefer the result. A line in the atlas records succession, not extinction.

**Lower cost is an opportunity, not proof of demand.** A falling cost shows where to look, not what people will choose. Travelers plan with AI but prefer to book through brands they trust; game studios adopt AI cautiously, mostly in production work; a generated world can be entered today and still cannot be kept. People weigh whole situations, including price, learning, habit and trust.

**People and institutions decide adoption, and who benefits.** Licensing and artist consent are shaping generative music; safety evidence and local permission pace robotaxis; regulators and central banks shape what a stablecoin may become; Wikipedia’s editors shape how it uses AI. The same capability under different institutions yields different chains.

Each pattern is traced, with its sources, in the histories below.

## How each chain reorganized around its want

Each history starts from its want and obstacles: what technology changed, how work and control reorganized, and which obstacle still binds.

### Knowledge: from answers to delegated inquiry

Every break in this chain attacked one of its obstacles and moved *where authority lived*. Oral knowledge lived in people. Libraries gathered external memory; print multiplied it. Search made finding nearly free but left the reader to compare pages and build the answer. Conversational systems began assembling it.

In 2026 a research agent can search, choose sources, synthesize them and revise its path while working; OpenAI’s [Deep Research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/) now supports connected sources, trusted-site restrictions and mid-course refinement. Finding and assembling have become cheap. Judging has not: who checks the synthesis, whose knowledge was admitted, which source deserves trust?

### Music: the branches never collapsed

Recording separated sound from the body producing it. Radio separated access from possession. Streaming put an enormous catalogue on demand and moved the scarcity from access to attention. Live performance, dancing and learning an instrument continued, because they serve wants a recording does not.

Generation added a branch: first a finished song from a wish, then an editable production environment. Suno’s [release history](https://suno.com/release-notes) traces the move to song editing, multitrack work and stem extraction, beyond rerolling a prompt. Licensing and artist consent are becoming part of the technical form, as in the [Warner Music Group–Suno agreement](https://www.wmg.com/news/warner-music-group-and-suno-forge-groundbreaking-partnership). Listening, performance, mastery and generative production coexist; music stands at no single stage.

### Maps: from representation to continuous guidance

The old obstacle was interpretation. A paper map represented a territory and left reading it, and the risk of a wrong turn, to the traveler. Route websites calculated a journey but still printed something like a map. Smartphone navigation moved the representation into the trip: location, traffic and missed turns now change the instruction continuously.

Google’s [Ask Maps and Immersive Navigation](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-immersive-navigation/) now combine map data with complex questions and real-time conditions: the chain has moved from an object, to a query, to a service accompanying movement. Beneath it, roads, businesses, public data and sensor networks remain a separately governed supply system. Guiding someone through the world is not producing the map of it.

### Software: from reusable code to delegated changes

The obstacle was always translation: intent had to become instructions that keep working. Programs became products; libraries reduced repeated work; open source distributed authorship; software-as-a-service changed delivery; GitHub made the repository a shared workplace. Coding assistance began by suggesting the next line.

Repository agents now accept a task, inspect a codebase, plan, edit, run checks and prepare work for review; [GitHub Copilot CLI](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-02-25-github-copilot-cli-is-now-generally-available/) describes modes from approving each action to more autonomous execution. The frontier is delegated engineering under tests and governance, not “say what you want and software appears.” Typing is no longer the scarce part. Saying exactly what is intended, and verifying that it was done, are.

### Gaming: participation arrived before generation

Play needs companions and a world worth returning to, and some players want to build that world. So gaming’s decisive transition was not from cartridges to digital stores but from a finished authored game to a maintained social system: multiplayer, live updates, modding, Minecraft and Roblox each weakened the line between producer and community.

In 2026, AI mainly lowers the cost of production work. Unity’s [AI tools](https://unity.com/blog/unity-ai-how-to-get-started) put ask, plan and agent workflows in the editor, and its [2026 industry report](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends) describes cautious adoption concentrated in coding and other production work. Fully generated, persistent worlds are not the ordinary product; moderation, discovery and platform economics still decide what survives.

### Retail: intelligence moves ahead of the warehouse

Each form attacked a different obstacle between the shopper and the thing. Mail order enlarged the shop without moving it. E-commerce moved the catalogue and the transaction online. Marketplaces gathered many sellers and made trust between strangers the platform’s job. Recommendation reorganized discovery around predicted preference.

The 2026 frontier is an agent acting across discovery, comparison, cart and checkout: Google’s [Universal Commerce Protocol](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/agentic-commerce-ai-tools-protocol-retailers-platforms/) tries to standardize those actions across agents and merchants, and Amazon’s [Alexa for Shopping](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus-ai-assistant-personalized-shopping-features) can monitor prices, add items and automate some purchases. The physical chain of making, stocking, picking, delivering and returning remains; the contest is increasingly over which merchant or agent holds the buyer’s intent.

### Video and film: watching and making split apart

Its two wants, to be absorbed in a story and to tell one’s own, traveled separate paths. The audience path ran from cinema to broadcast, home video, streaming and recommendation feeds, moving the scarcity from access to attention. The production path ran from expensive studios to digital editing, cheap distribution and creator platforms; YouTube and TikTok changed who could publish without making watching equivalent to making.

Generation is changing the production branch again: Runway’s [Agent](https://runwayml.com/news/introducing-runway-agent) goes from conversational direction to multi-shot video with a timeline for intervention, and Adobe’s [2026 Firefly and Premiere updates](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/04/15/adobe-extends-leadership-video-unleashing-new-ai-powered-creation-firefly-reinventing-color-editors-in-premiere) combine generation with editing. Audiences still mostly receive finished sequences. One line for video would hide this split.

### Photography: capture and synthesis now coexist

One machine once served two wants: keeping a moment as it was, and making an image as one imagines it. Roll film lowered the expertise needed to capture; instant film shortened the wait; digital capture removed film and chemistry; phones joined capture to computation, editing and distribution.

Synthetic images opened a separate branch for the second want, now moving from prompting toward exact, reversible control, as Adobe’s [Precision Flow and AI Markup](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/04/09/new-image-editing-features-adobe-firefly-get-you-from-almost-there-to-exactly-right) illustrate: marking regions, preserving chosen elements, refining rather than accepting a sealed result. The first want gained an obstacle in return. When anyone can make a convincing image, a photograph’s worth as evidence rests on authenticity and provenance, which the chain must now supply.

### Personal transport: availability and operation diverged

Owning a car answered the want by joining access to a vehicle with the work of driving, storing and maintaining it. Taxis separated ownership from the journey. Ride-hailing improved dispatch and availability, but a human still drove each trip: the driver remained the binding cost.

Robotaxis change operation. In its [May 2026 update](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/05/welcoming-riders-in-the-ojai/), Waymo reported more than 20 million fully autonomous trips across at least eleven cities. Yet private cars, transit, cycling, taxis, ride-hailing and bounded autonomous fleets coexist, each serving a different part of the want. The frontier is a capital-intensive, regulated fleet whose expansion depends on safety evidence, local coverage and public permission.

### Encyclopedias: a human source beneath machine answers

Trust is this want’s oldest obstacle, and each form answered it differently. Printed encyclopedias concentrated expert editing into a durable household reference; CD-ROM reduced the bulk and eased search. Wikipedia changed production itself: a continuously revised commons written and governed by volunteers. Search engines presented fragments of it without changing how it was made.

AI answer systems now add a presentation layer above it, while Wikimedia’s [AI strategy](https://wikimediafoundation.org/news/2025/04/30/our-new-ai-strategy-puts-wikipedias-humans-first/) uses AI to assist translation, moderation and routine work and keeps editorial judgment human. The chain has three layers, a human-curated source, automated maintenance and machine-generated answers, each with its own incentives and standards of accountability. A reader’s trust is now earned, or not, layer by layer.

### Food: planning digitized; matter did not

Food answers wants that information cannot meet on its own, and it never followed a single line from cooking to delivery. Households, restaurants, industrial producers, supermarkets, takeout, meal kits and delivery platforms serve different mixes of nourishment, convenience, craft and ritual. Home cooking survives each convenience partly because, for many people, some of the effort is the point. A recipe is information; a meal is not.

The latest change compresses planning and procurement: Instacart’s [2026 assistant](https://company.instacart.com/updates/instacarts-ai-assistant-powered-by-14-years-of-grocery-expertise) can turn preferences, photographs or ideas into a meal plan and a ready-to-buy cart. Substitution, perishability, picking, delivery and cooking remain. The chain has reached from intent into the basket; it has not turned food into information.

### Money and payments: coexistence rather than succession

Every form of money serves the same want, and trust is its binding obstacle. Cash, deposits, cards, transfers, wallets and instant payments did not simply replace one another; each changed a different layer: the claim people hold, the interface, the message, or how institutions settle. Cryptoassets and stablecoins added issuers and rails, not one accepted next form of money.

In 2026, tokenized deposits, stablecoins and agent-initiated payments are developing alongside conventional systems. The [BIS Annual Economic Report 2026](https://www.bis.org/press/p260623.htm) finds that current stablecoin designs fall short of the properties that ensure trust in money, singleness in particular, and places tokenization within the existing two-tier system. The contest is institutional and over trust; this series’ money essays trace it through [settlement](/posts/battle_for_dollar_supremacy), [the hidden cost of money](/posts/the_price_you_cannot_see) and [the spendable form](/posts/the_spendable_form).

### Travel and lodging: planning advances faster than accountability

Each form attacked a different obstacle: travel agencies assembled fragmented information and booked through reservation networks; online agencies exposed comparison and booking to the traveler; peer marketplaces added lodging supply, and new platform governance with it.

AI is changing inspiration, comparison and itinerary-building first. In Expedia’s company-commissioned [2026 survey](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Expedia-Group-Reveals-The-AI-Trust-Gap-Travelers-Embrace-AI-for-Planning-but-Rely-on-Trusted-Brands-to-Book/default.aspx), about half of travelers were comfortable letting AI suggest options, but two-thirds said they would not trust an AI assistant to book anything on their behalf. Airbnb’s [2026 release](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-2026-summer-release/) similarly expands services and AI-supported comparison around its marketplace. The frontier is an AI planning layer on platforms that remain accountable for inventory, payment, refunds and failures. The cheap part of the want moved first; the part that needs someone to answer for it has not.

### Virtual places: generation before persistence

A place to be with others has to outlast any single visit. Virtual places began as text spaces held together by language and convention; graphical worlds added continuous visual space; Second Life, Minecraft and Roblox developed different forms of persistence and construction; social VR added presence. The place was produced by software and social activity, not surveyed from a territory.

World models now generate interactive environments in response to action. DeepMind’s [Genie 3](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/) produces navigable worlds in real time, but for only a few minutes of continuous interaction and a constrained range of actions. Multi-user consistency, ownership, interoperability and creator rights remain unresolved. The frontier is a generated simulation, not yet a place a community can keep living in after the prompt ends.

## A living atlas

Set side by side, the fourteen histories do not end in the same place, and nothing obliges them to. What they share is the order that explains them. Human tendencies change slowest. The wants they produce — to understand, to arrive, to be fed, to play, to belong, to trust — outlast every form that has served them. Technology changes capability and constraint, and each time an obstacle falls the scarce thing moves: from finding to judging, from access to attention, from capture to belief, from dispatch to operation, from typing to verifying, from planning to accountability. The chain reorganizes around the same want each time.

**The value system endures while the value chains reorganize. Wants change slowest; implementations change fastest.**

So the green nodes should keep moving: they are the forms visible in July 2026, not destinations. Judge the next change in any chain by what it does for the want and which obstacle it leaves standing, not by someone else’s stages. [The Law](/posts/the_selection_pressure) gives the engine beneath this atlas: the want is constant, cost selects among methods, and the rent relocates to whichever cost has not yet fallen. A companion essay, [De DAW of the Future](/posts/the_daw_of_the_future), applies the same order to one chain in depth: making music.
